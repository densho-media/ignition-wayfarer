## H-1 "The cadence problem" (era E1)

**Narrates:** VTX-01C target factory, VTX-01B rep-rated drivers, VTX-05A engagement. Credibility **B**.

The first ignitions were single events, a shot a day. The hard part of fusion propulsion turned out not to be lighting a target but
lighting two hundred of them a second for years, each one engaged to a few hundredths of a millimetre. A proposed history: the industry
that solved this was the terrestrial inertial-fusion power plant, whose economics needed hundreds of thousands of targets a day; the
drive inherited its factories, its injectors and its control software. The Vertex target factory is that plant's descendant, scaled to
millions of targets a day and drawing well under a thousandth of the fusion power.

*Physical manifestation (for T05):* target factory and magazines in the equipment module; two injectors; tracking stations along the
pellet path. All PARAMETRIC_PLACEHOLDER.

## H-2 "Hot iron" (era E2)

**Narrates:** VTX-07A split warm loop, VTX-07C house load. Credibility **B**.

Every ship before the Vertex line carried radiator wings kilometres wide, because its machinery could not tolerate heat. The quiet
revolution was in power electronics and magnetic cores that run at 700-800 K, where a radiator sheds ten times more per square metre
than at 400 K. A proposed history: this came from industries with no interest in space (deep geothermal, hypersonic flight, Venus
landers), and the shipwrights adopted it late. In the model it is the single largest engineering saving: 600 to 800 K takes a V1-class
ship from about 13 kt to 9.5 kt and the wing tip from 1,461 m to 366 m.

*Physical manifestation:* two warm wings shrink from km scale to a few hundred metres; a second warm manifold; split radiator bands.

## H-3 "The coupling threshold" (era E3) — declared breakthrough

**Narrates:** VTX-01D D-T spark, VTX-01A fuel-only D-³He target, VTX-01. Credibility **B in kind, declared in degree** (D-T06-1).

Physics allows D-³He to burn, but squeezing fuel costs energy, so a driver can only win as much gain as its compression efficiency
allows. A proposed history in two steps, both consistent with known physics. First came D-T sparks that light D-³He burn waves, accepting
a little tritium for the sake of ignition at all. Second, and slower, came the long climb in coupling: the share of driver energy that
ends up in the fuel at the lowest entropy. The story must say plainly that the coupling reached about a third to a half. That is the one
event in the Vertex history that is chosen, not derived. The model shows why it matters: at a fifth, the Vertex ship exists only with
every other improvement in place, and at a tenth it is a 30 kt vessel.

*Physical manifestation:* nothing visible at the burn. A target-formation coil sits at the injector muzzle (CONCEPTUAL_ILLUSTRATION).
The pulses are larger and slower than FDP-1's: about 44 t TNT at 57-94 Hz in V3, against 19.5 t at 212 Hz.

*Not narrated:* where kilotonnes of ³He come from. That is a fuel-economy history (lunar regolith, gas-giant atmospheres) for the owner
to place.

## H-4 "Rings that remember" (era E4)

**Narrates:** VTX-02A canon Fiji card, VTX-02B no-insulation REBCO loops, VTX-03 pulsed nozzle. Credibility **C** (Fiji, canon) and
**B**; nozzle validation **declared** in degree (D-T06-2).

Fiji does in this history exactly what canon says and nothing more: it amplifies a slowly applied seed field up to thirty-fold and holds
the shape of the field between pulses. Its meaning for a pulsed drive is practical: ring-scale nozzle loops a hundred metres across need
far fewer ampere-turns, and the field geometry does not wander between bursts. Canon's own start sequence already tells the moment:
first fusion, then "the lattice enters activation range", then "field lock develops". A proposed history: field lock is what the crews
listen for. What Fiji does not do is make the nozzle efficient; that came from test stands and from field-design methods first used to
shape the twisted magnets of stellarators. The history should say a pulsed nozzle reached near its theoretical efficiency only if the
owner declares it (D-T06-2), because simulations today reach 0.34-0.70 against the 0.79 the ship needs.

*Physical manifestation:* three loops in twelve sectors, Fiji carriers on the burn-shadowed outer band, sector joints clocked loop to
loop (the visible helix, VTX-03A) if T03 finds that it helps.

## H-5 "Convergence" (era E5)

**Narrates:** VTX-01B, T06-NEW-1, T06-NEW-2, VTX-07B, VTX-06B. Credibility **B**.

No single invention made the Vertex ship possible. A proposed history: in each generation the shipwrights took one more improvement
from another industry (lighter drivers, nanotube laminates, liquid radiators, thinner refractory pipes), and each one saved only a few
per cent. Then the sum crossed a line. A ship that could not close, or that would have weighed 160 kt, became a ship of about 10 kt.
The model is honest about the limit: payload, propellant and tanks set a floor of about 4.2 kt at the mission exhaust velocity, and no
drive improvement moves it.

*Physical manifestation:* wings of a few hundred metres instead of kilometres; thinner hoops and spars; at coupling 0.3, the nozzle's
outer loop at about r 128-171 m, wider than FDP-1's 112 m, because the consistent pulses are larger.

## H-6 "The unfinished problem" (all eras)

**Narrates:** VTX-08, 08A, 08B, 08D. Credibility **B / UNRESOLVED**.

Every history above ends with the same open question: a ship braking at 0.016 c flies engine-first into interstellar dust at 4,800 km/s,
and no shield physics has been tested within a factor of hundreds of that speed. A proposed history should keep this visible as a real
risk the crews know about, not a solved problem. T02's results will decide whether it becomes an engineering story, a mission-profile
story or a declared one.
