# T04 report: fusion ignition, target design and radiation conversion

Research, not canon. Every number is from `results/ignition.json` (generated by `ignition.py`) or a source in `registry_proposal.toml`;
labels as in `planning/HANDOFF.md`. Stages below are developmental hypotheses, not dates.

## Summary

The Vertex design lives above a target-gain cliff of about 705 (V1). T04 finds that the cliff is not only a gain problem but an
**energy-consistency** problem: a compression-driven target cannot have a gain larger than eta_c b eps / (alpha eps_F(rho)), because the
driver must at least supply the Fermi-degenerate energy of the compressed fuel. At FDP-1's 3,000 g/cm3 that ceiling is 1,787 x eta_c/alpha,
so FDP-1's gain 3,000 would need more energy in the fuel than the driver delivers (eta_c/alpha = 1.68). The V1 cliff needs eta_c/alpha
0.41 at 3,000 g/cm3 and 0.20 at 1,000 g/cm3; lower density buys coupling margin with bigger, slower pulses and a heavier ship.

D-3He ignition itself needs no unknown physics: charged-product heating beats bremsstrahlung above about 33 keV, and a hot spot of about
7 g/cm2 at 50 keV self-heats (11x the D-T hot-spot rhoR). It is undemonstrated. The most credible ignition route is a fast-ignited D-T
booster core, whose cost is tritium (0.04-0.14 t per mission), not neutrons. Magnetisation helps only above B R about 1.2-2 MG cm.

**How far credible physics gets:** D-3He burn with a D-T booster at fuel rhoR about 90 g/cm2 is consistent with published ICF physics.
**What remains:** gain about 700 at 3-100 Hz, which demands a driver-to-fuel coupling of 0.13-0.41 (density 500-3,000 g/cm3), against
present ablative implosions in the percent range (engineering judgment; see Unknowns). No known compression scheme reaches that; it is
the explicit breakthrough the design depends on (section 6). **Next experiment:** a 1-D radiation-hydrodynamic burn calculation of a
D-T-cored D-3He sphere at 1,000-3,000 g/cm3 (section 9).

## 2. Reproduction and burn-model checks

`ignition.py` reproduces the Atlas gain cliffs exactly (1,257 / 705 / 581) and the liner table to machine precision before extending.

**H_B.** The calculator's H_B for D-3He at 100 keV is 44.2 g/cm2, 13 % below the published minimum 51; its own D-T minimum is 5.36
against the published 7.3, so the temperature shape is optimistic by about 25 %. Sensitivity (V1): with H_B at the published minimum,
departure 10,416 -> 10,959 t; matching the D-T minimum, 11,828 t (fuel rhoR 123 g/cm2). The gain cliff stays 705 within the bisection
resolution: H_B moves mass, not the cliff. Recommendation for T00: report the published-H_B case beside V1 (no calculator change).

## 5. Radiation and energy ledger implications

Per-pulse ledger and spectra are in `EARLY.md` section 3. The neutral escape is X-ray dominated (1.15 % of fusion against 5.5e-6
neutrons); a D-T booster core adds less than 2e-7 of escaping 14.1 MeV neutrons, so it changes no shield (T02) and no hot loop (T01).
Isotopically tailored layers (VTX-04D) in the target can only recover the escaping neutron share, at most about 5.5e-6 of fusion: no
propulsive value; their value stays in the shield (secondary gammas), which is T02's. Tritium bred in a Li-6 layer of the target would leave
with the exhaust. The tamper-inertia case adds no energy, only confinement time; nothing is credited twice.

**Hydrogen low gear (VTX-04B, D-04).** At fixed jet power the low gear multiplies thrust by sqrt(1 + mu): at most 2x at mu = 3, for a
four-burn mass ratio 2.72x larger. The phases where more thrust could matter are short: escape spirals from a 400 km Earth orbit take
1.3 days at 0.0069 g (0.65 days at mu = 3), from GEO 0.5 days, solar escape from 1 AU 2.1 days; a 100 km/s course change 17 days (8.5).
The drive cannot be lit near a station or planet anyway (19.5 t TNT per pulse, 20 % of fusion as plume). Against this, the idle vessels
cost 667 t at V1 departure. **Argument for keeping them:** a non-propulsive use (water/hydrogen shielding, a mission reserve) that T02 may
value. **Argument against:** no propulsive phase gains more than about a day. Recommendation (not a decision): remove the H2 low gear;
D-04 stays ApeDevil's.

**Burn diagnostics and safe stop (VTX-05B).** Pulse period 4.7 ms (V0) / 9.3 ms (V1). Per pulse the ship must measure: target track and
timing before the driver fires (the only safe stop: do not fire), X-ray yield and the B-dot signal in the loops (the recovery pickup is
also the diamagnetic diagnostic), neutron yield (a D-T core makes 14.1 MeV a clean ignition flag), and beam energy per line. Light reaches
loop 1 in 0.12 us, debris in about 2 us, so detection is never the limit; decisions act on the next pulse. A dud puts 27-115 MJ of driver
energy into the target and 400 km/s debris; recovery gets nothing, so pulse storage must hold at least one or two pulses of driver energy
(60 MJ wall plug at gain 3,000, 255 MJ at 705) to continue, and N consecutive duds must stop the train. A 10 % asymmetric pulse carries
about 660 N s sideways against 6,600 N s along the axis: an attitude, not a structural, load.

## 6. Required breakthroughs: engineering against physics

| Need | Physics-limited or engineering-limited | Named breakthrough |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D-3He ignition | established theory, never demonstrated; booster core or 7 g/cm2 / 50 keV hot spot | demonstrated D-3He burn from a D-T core (engineering + experiment) |
| Gain about 700 | **bounded by energy conservation through compression**: needs eta_c/alpha 0.13-0.41 | **high-efficiency near-isentropic compression of fuel-only targets** at 13-41 % driver-to-fuel coupling, which no known scheme reaches; engineering in principle, beyond any demonstrated mechanism |
| FDP-1's gain 3,000 at 3,000 g/cm3 | impossible by compression alone (eta_c/alpha 1.68 > 1) | would need a **Level C compression aid** (D-T04-1) |
| 100-200 Hz targets | engineering (injection 5-9 km/s, factory, survival) | one-target-in-sky delivery (T04-NEW-4) |
| Tritium for the booster | logistics (0.04-0.14 t per mission, 27 % decays) | a tritium-breeding economy at home; nothing onboard breeds enough |

The Level C option, stated so it is not hidden: a compression aid must put 0.4-1.7 times the driver's beam energy into the fuel as
internal energy each pulse, independently of the driver's ablation physics. The canon Fiji card (slow amplification of an applied seed
field) does not do this; adding it is a new Fiji property and falls under D-07. T04 recommends option A of D-T04-1: accept
physics-consistent targets.

## 9. Next experiment

A 1-D Lagrangian radiation-hydrodynamics burn calculation (open codes such as MULTI-IFE or HYADES-class, or a written 1-D code in the lab)
of a D-T-cored, fuel-only D-3He sphere at 1,000 and 3,000 g/cm3, isochoric, fast ignited: does the burn propagate, what burn fraction
and peak temperature result, how does it compare with b = rhoR/(rhoR + H_B), and what ignitor energy is needed. Output: burn fraction and
yield per ignitor energy, replacing this report's 0-D boundary and its 3.5x calibration. Cost: a thread-week; needs no MHD/PIC (not D-09).

## 10. Limitations

0-D hot-spot model (no PdV, Krokhin-Rozanov deposition, Spitzer conduction, optically thin bremsstrahlung), calibrated on D-T by one
factor; proton range by electron-drag scaling from the D-T alpha fit; Fermi floor ignores the ablator and the hot spot's own pressure;
the ceiling is a bound, not a design. Coupling values for present schemes are engineering judgment, not a sourced number. Tamper inertia
is thin-shell, constant-pressure (k band). Nothing here is a transport, hydrodynamic or burn simulation.
