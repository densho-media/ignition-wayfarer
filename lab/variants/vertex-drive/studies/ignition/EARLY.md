## 1. What changes for the other threads

1. **The FDP-1 optimistic target is not energy-consistent.** Compressing its 0.344 g of D-3He to 3,000 g/cm3 costs at least 45.6 MJ
   (Fermi-degenerate floor, alpha = 1), more than the 27 MJ the driver delivers at gain 3,000. A compression-driven target's gain is
   bounded by G <= 1,787 x eta_c / alpha at that density (eta_c: driver energy to fuel internal energy). MODEL.
2. **The gain cliff barely moves with density (705-719 for V1 from 500 to 3,000 g/cm3), but the coupling it needs does:** eta_c/alpha
   0.41 at 3,000, 0.32 at 2,000, 0.26 at 1,500, 0.20 at 1,000, 0.13 at 500 g/cm3. Lower density means larger, slower pulses and a much
   heavier ship (V1 at gain 3,000: 10.4 kt at 3,000 g/cm3, 23.9 kt at 1,000, 64.2 kt at 500). MODEL.
3. **With a consistent gain the V1 ship stops being 10 kt.** At eta_c/alpha = 0.3 (itself far beyond any present compression scheme):
   3,000 g/cm3 does not close (ceiling 517 < cliff 705); 1,500 g/cm3 closes at 96.6 kt; 1,000 g/cm3 at 59.5 kt; 500 g/cm3 at 92.9 kt.
   T01 and T06: treat V1/V2 masses as conditional on the 3,000 gain, which needs eta_c/alpha 1.68. MODEL.
4. **D-T spark costs tritium, not neutrons.** A D-T core of rhoR 2-3 g/cm2 inside the 90 g/cm2 fuel releases 0.3-1.3 MJ (enough to light
   the D-3He hot spot), escaping 14.1 MeV neutrons stay below 2e-7 of fusion (D-D today: 5.5e-6). Tritium: 43-144 kg loaded per mission.
5. **The next target in flight is destroyed at FDP-1's 400 m/s.** 23 targets are in the burn's sky at once; the next sits 1.9 m from the
   burn. One-target-in-sky delivery needs 9.2 km/s at 212 Hz (V0), about 4.6 km/s at 107 Hz (V1). T01 owns the injector (VTX-01C).

For T02: the X-ray share (1.15 %) dominates every neutral channel by a factor of about 2,000. A D-T spark core does not change the
neutron share materially; only an outside D-T primary (two-stage target, rejected) would (0.2-0.45 % of fusion as 14.1 MeV).

For T03: the debris is a fully ionised expanding sphere at 2.4 MeV/u mean, about 1.3x that at the edge; a dud brings 400 km/s debris
with no field compression. Larger designs (C, D) keep the same velocity spectrum and scale energy x9 (C) and x36 (D) per pulse at
1/9 and 1/36 the rate; X-ray stand-off scales as sqrt(yield): 130 m (C), 259 m (D) from the calculator.

For T01: driver energy per pulse at the V1 cliff is 115 MJ (A) on target; wall plug / 0.45. The target factory's energy is not
computed here; a design-C target (3.1 g) is 9x the fuel per pulse at 1/9 the rate.

## 4. Burn model checks (no calculator change)

- H_B: the calculator's D-3He 44.2 g/cm2 (100 keV) is 13 % below the published minimum 51 (Atzeni & Meyer-ter-Vehn, secondary). Using 51
  raises V1 departure 10,416 -> 10,959 t; matching the published D-T minimum (H_B 60 at 100 keV) gives 11,828 t. The gain cliff stays at
  705 within the bisection resolution. Spin polarisation lowers V1 to 9,476 t.
- Tamper inertia (new case beside the Atlas liner table, which is reproduced exactly): with the full thin-shell inertia (k = 1) the best
  tamper adds +1.2 % ve at rhoR 90 g/cm2 (mu 0.03) and +10.7 % at 1 g/cm2; at k = 0.5, +0.3 % and +3.0 %. D-02 holds to within about 1 %.
