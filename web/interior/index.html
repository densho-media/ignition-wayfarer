EXPLORE INTERIOR

Stand inside the spacecraft as a crew trainee, on the deck of habitat ring A. Wayfarer is coasting and the ring turns at 4 rpm, so the deck presses up at about half of Earth's gravity: there is no other gravity on board. Walk the curved corridor, run, jump and feel how a rotating floor behaves. When you want the whole journey, the full excursion starts at the development station and takes you through the airlock, the transfer drum and down the ladder into the ring.

The engine and the ship package download when you press Start.

Controls

  • Your keys: on your first visit Wayfarer asks which key you want for each move, starting with Which key would you like to use to move forward?, then back, left, right, jump and run. Press the key you want, or click Use suggested controls. Your choices stay in this browser and are used by Explore Exterior too.
  • On the deck: click the view to look around (Esc frees the cursor) · your move keys walk, relative to the camera · your run key with forward runs · jump is a push of the legs (no second jump in the air). Use, ship operations, view and the ring map are listed with their keys on screen.
  • Controls (bottom left, or F10) shows every action with your key and changes any of them at once: change, add a second key, remove, swap on a conflict, set up again.
  • Touch: left thumb walks, right thumb drags to look; the Use, Ops, View and Map buttons appear when they apply. Landscape works best.
  • Back and exit: ← Exit (bottom left) leaves and unloads the engine; your browser's Back button does the same.

About this experience

  • The station-to-ring excursion (EXC-001): the ship's physics, ring rotation, gravity transitions, hatches and operating modes are the project's own simulation, running in the browser build of the native Godot project.
  • SIMULATION_ONLY: the ring-ramp time-lapse and the closure timings are display choices; no transfer timing, procedure or interlock is released. Learning stations in ring A explain what you see.
  • Spin gravity, not a gravity setting: the deck's pull comes from the ring's rotation (about 0.49 g at the feet), and moving bodies feel its Coriolis part. A jump of 1.4 m/s rises about 0.20 m and lands a few centimetres off the take-off spot.
  • You are the newcomer, the project's MPFB character. Physics moves the body; the walk, run, jump, fall and landing clips follow its measured speed, so the feet do not slide. Speeds, the jump and the camera are PROTOTYPE values (docs/LOCOMOTION.md).
  • Tested in Chromium-based desktop browsers and in Chrome's phone and tablet emulation with touch; real phones, Firefox and Safari are not yet verified. On phones the 3-D view renders at a reduced, frame-rate-adaptive resolution.
