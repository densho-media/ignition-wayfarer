EXPLORE INTERIOR

Enter the spacecraft as a crew trainee. You start in the development station docked to Wayfarer's nose: work the airlock, board the ship, ask it to undock and spin its habitat rings up, ride the transfer drum, climb down into spin gravity and walk a whole ring round, through every sector and corridor, before you come back the same way. One continuous run under your control, with the ship's instructor pointing out what to do next.

The engine and the ship package download when you press Start.

Controls

  • Desktop: click the view to look around (Esc frees the cursor) · W/S move along the line or walk, A/D step sideways · E use what you face (hatch, door, drum, ladder, the ring branch) · O ship operations at a trunk panel (undock, spin the rings up) · V view your body · M ring map · F10 all controls and rebinding.
  • Touch: left thumb moves where the body can walk or climb, right thumb drags to look; the Use, Ops, View and Map buttons appear when they apply. Landscape works best.
  • Where to go: the goal at the top left always says what to do next and which key does it. Guided objectives lead you through the ship, and you are free to wander: every door that is open can be walked through, and shut hatches and doors open with Use.
  • Back and exit: the way back to the station is the way you came. ← Exit (bottom left) leaves and unloads the engine; your browser's Back button does the same.

About this experience

  • The station-to-ring excursion (EXC-001): the ship's physics, ring rotation, gravity transitions, hatches and operating modes are the project's own simulation, running in the browser build of the native Godot project.
  • SIMULATION_ONLY: the ring-ramp time-lapse and the closure timings are display choices; no transfer timing, procedure or interlock is released. Learning stations in ring A explain what you see.
  • The trainee appears as a proxy body; the native development runtime carries the full characters.
  • Tested in Chromium-based desktop browsers and in Chrome's phone and tablet emulation with touch; real phones, Firefox and Safari are not yet verified. On phones the 3-D view renders at a reduced, frame-rate-adaptive resolution.
