## 2026-10-31 (pass 5): 1.5 g egress past a deployed care station, head-up turn

Decisions by Claude under the owner's delegation of 2026-10-31 (research-based, PROTOTYPE). No geometry change.

**Egress past the 1.5 g care station** (`work/project-thread-1ysr1f/d02_egress.py`, screen plane, all three sector functions; the station
re-ranked on care clearance and egress because locked 1.5 g has no routine transfer, 15!A197). Care point Burn_Couch_1, attendants on the
aisle side (the couch lockers fill the wall side), kneeling. Every two-attendant layout searched (full side strip, torso strip, second
attendant beside the head attendant, at the foot end, at the head on the wall side; head-end shift -0.3..+0.3 m) leaves 0.534 m between
the side attendant and Burn_Couch_2 (-0.066 m against the 0.60 m forward-facing walking body). With only the airway attendant in place the
aisle clears +0.08 m. Reading:
- The 0.60 m walking body is a forward-facing shoulder width. A short pass beside a kneeling attendant is a sidestep: 95th percentile
  male bideltoid breadth is about 0.53 m and chest depth about 0.29 m, and OSHA accepts an 18 in (0.457 m) walkway for maintenance access
  (OSHA interpretation letter 1982-03-09). The 0.534 m gap passes that criterion by +0.077 m.
- Procedure: when someone must pass in 1.5 g, the side attendant sits back on the heels or moves to the head end for the seconds of the
  pass (the aisle then clears +0.08 m for a forward-facing body); the airway attendant stays.
- Result: PASS as a constricted, sidestep passage (+0.077 m) in the work/medical, Garden and habitation sectors; a forward-facing pass
  needs the side attendant to yield. The habitation -0.25 m in the old screen came from choosing Burn_Couch_2 as the care point; with
  Burn_Couch_1 all three sectors read the same.

**Head-up turn of a spinally immobilised patient** (the door turn in the corridor transfer, rotating ring or stopped; never under the
1.5 g burn). Accepted with conditions:
- Head-up (reverse Trendelenburg) positioning is used for head-injured patients because it does not raise intracranial pressure the way
  head-down does (nursing references; LITFL review of Trendelenburg for hypotension, which advises against head-down in shock).
- Vertical and steep head-up packaging of spinal patients is routine in confined-space rescue (Sked and Spec-Pak with a spine splint), with
  vertical hang time kept under 5-7 min (Roco Rescue) and vertical lifting of suspected spinal injuries avoided when another way exists
  (Life Support International). The harness-suspension literature gives ~5 min as the conservative limit for motionless upright
  suspension.
- The corridor turn takes under a minute and happens at ring gravity (0.5 g or less) or in 0 g, so the orthostatic load is at most half
  of Earth's.
- Conditions: full-length rigid support under the vacuum mattress (a vacuum mattress alone sags when lifted by its ends), foot plate or
  crotch strap against sliding, head up only, under 1 min upright, blood pressure watched; a hypotensive patient (systolic under 90 mmHg)
  is not tilted and stays at the care point until the ring is stopped (0 g, no tilt needed).
