Primary structure, Phase II: real mass, real loads, real failure

Phase II sizes the H, HC, A and C concepts in a verified free-free frame model under thrust-bounded loads, tests six against three rails by breaking them, and ties the result to ISSA's systems layout. Why the hub path

Routing the primary load through the rotor bay on the spine family with hub cones avoids the cage-end kink that the bypass collar must turn. Every concept is 290 to 830 t lighter on the hub path, and no bypass variant closes without members at their maximum size.

Why H rather than C

C is lighter (1,054 t) but rests on speculative materials and is kept as the benchmark (D06). A is the all-metal control at 1,318 t. H uses established Ti and Al-Li in the primary path, and HC adds carbon longerons where the import budget allows.

Why G matters

Moving 30 % of the H2 share aft (X −70 to −20 m, He-3 and D2 stay protected forward) shortens the heavy-nose lever. It is the only lever that raised λ and lowered mass at the same time. ISSA owns the tank layout, so this is a joint sensitivity, not a proposal.

What is not closed

λ below 1 means the linear model predicts divergence below limit thrust. Stiffness alone does not cure it (the static cantilever bound needs about 6x the rail area). Phase III must test mass placement, active TVC/GNC, damping and a nonlinear time-domain model before any morphology is frozen.

E · SECTION 3.3

Global stability and modes

Global stability was computed for a fixed-direction (static) thrust, a full follower, partial followers and a follower with a simple TVC gain, with the thrust applied at the twelve thrust-ring nodes. λ is the multiple of limit thrust at which the linear model diverges or flutters. The target is 1.4 / 0.75 = 1.87.

Every variant is stability-critical

λ lies between 0.15 and 0.82 for all variants and all thrust models. H hub F1: 0.71 static, 0.68 follower, 0.52 with TVC gain 1, 0.59 / 0.52 with joint compliance 0.7. The basis is converged (30 to 140 modes change λ by less than 2 %).

The analytic bounds agree

From the rail EI with L = 243 m: the static heavy-nose cantilever bound gives 0.3 to 0.45, the Beck follower bound 2.4 to 3.7. The FE results sit between them, as they should for a heavy-nose free-free ship.

What moves λ

G (30 % H2 aft) raises H to 0.82, the only lever that also saves mass. Rail area does not scale well: closing the static bound needs about 6x the rail area. A naive TVC loop lowers λ, so control must be designed with the structure.

What Phase III needs

A nonlinear time-domain model with the real thrust law, slosh, damping and a GNC loop, and mass placement studied jointly with ISSA. Until then the morphology is not frozen.

F · D03 SECTION 8

Three versus six rails, damage and repair

D03: six rails in three paired sectors against three rails. Each variant was damaged by removing a rail bay, a rail joint, the shared sector node, both rails of a sector, a pickup, a collar chord and a keel bay, then re-solved with mass transfer and classified.

Six rails are lighter intact

H (six) 1,176 t, H3 (three) 1,310 t: the paired sectors give a deeper section and shorter diagonals.

As strength-sized, damage tolerance is poor

Most rail-bay and joint losses class COAST-ONLY or IMMEDIATE REPAIR because the diagonals next to the loss overload. Pickup, collar-chord and keel losses keep reduced thrust. A shared sector node is a common-mode failure (334 members overloaded in H). Utilisations in the hundreds or thousands mean the damaged Warren bay has become a mechanism: it has a single shear path, so the fail-safe districts need X-braced bays.

Emergency and nominal thrust are separate

A damaged ship is checked at 50 % thrust with factor 1.0 (emergency) and at 100 % with 1.4 (nominal). These give different penalties and different classes, and are never merged.

Reading the penalty. The NOMINAL target for H costs +134 t (12 %) and moves every single rail-bay loss to REDUCED THRUST with full emergency thrust available; a lost joint still leaves the ship COAST-ONLY, so joints need twin-lug fail-safe fittings (Phase III). The EMERGENCY resizing does not change the outcome: member sizing alone does not make a 3-rail or 6-rail ship limp home after a rail loss without fail-safe joints. Six rails win on both intact and redundant mass.
